# Spike: is BS 2084 ready to verify?

Date: 2026-10-04 (Ashwin 2083). Status: **not verified. Strong evidence that our converter's 2084 data is wrong.** See D-111.

## Question

Can BS 2084 be added to the verified years (D-014), so that terms may run past Chaitra 2083 (D-110)?

## What could not be checked

This cloud environment's network policy blocks every Nepali calendar site: hamropatro.com (and english.hamropatro.com), nepalipatro.com.np, ashesh.com.np, nepcalendar.com, nepalicalendar.rat32.com, englishnepalidate.com and timeanddate.com. It also blocks the government calendar committee at npns.gov.np. Search-engine summaries got through, but they are not a source. The official almanac for a year is normally decided shortly before the year begins, so 2084 pages online today may be projections anyway (bs-dates.md).

## Method 2: astronomy (`bs-year-check/check.py`)

A BS month begins at a sankranti, the sun's entry into a sidereal sign. Nepal's almanac computes the sankranti the traditional way, so its month starts differ from modern astronomy in two ways:
- a month-dependent offset, which is the classical equation of centre;
- a slow drift, because the Surya Siddhanta year is 3.45 minutes longer than the sidereal year.

The script computes every sidereal (Lahiri) sankranti from 1943 with Swiss Ephemeris and compares them with the 1,008 verified month starts in `bs-golden.json`.

- **Test A, no fitting.** For each month, it measures the hours between the sankranti and the month's first midnight over BS 2000 to 2083. Kartik, Mangsir and Magh never started more than 16 hours before the sankranti. Our converter's 2084 starts them 26 to 28 hours before, a whole day earlier than anything in 84 years. 5 of its 13 month starts fall outside the range seen.
- **Test B, fitted.** A per-month offset plus one yearly drift reproduces every verified month except BS 2062 Jestha, the boundary the public calendars dispute (`DISPUTED_WINDOWS`).
  - Only drifts of 3.45 to 3.75 minutes a year fit, and theory says 3.45, so the model is Nepal's method in effect, not a curve.
  - Fitted on BS 2000 to 2069 alone, it predicts all 168 months of BS 2070 to 2083.
  - Every setting that fits gives the same 2084 answer: 8 of our converter's 13 month starts are a day early. That covers Asar, Bhadra, Ashwin, Kartik, Mangsir, Poush and Magh, and Baisakh 1 of 2085.
- **The other family** (31 32 31 32 31 30 30 30 29 29 30 31) agrees with Test B on all 13 month starts. On Test A it falls outside the seen range at one boundary only: Baisakh 2085 lands 2 hours past the edge, which the drift explains.

One search summary gave Kartik 1, 2084 as 17 October 2027, which is our converter's date. Its page could not be opened, and calendar sites commonly draw future years from these same libraries.

## Conclusion

- 2084 stays unverified. The gate did its job: had 2084 been opened on our converter's data, about half the year's dates would be stored a day early.
- When the official 2084 calendar is published, expect it to differ from `@inicrea/bikram-sambat-core` 0.1.3. Verifying it will then need converter data that matches the official calendar: a library upgrade or a replacement library. The golden test must pass for 2000 to 2083 and be extended by 2084.
- Re-run the check on any candidate year: `pip install pyswisseph`, then `python3 docs/spikes/bs-year-check/check.py 2084 <12 month lengths>`. It takes about two minutes. It is evidence, never a source: a year is verified only against the published calendar (CLAUDE.md section 6).
